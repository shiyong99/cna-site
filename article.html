Around 100 businesses caught buying fake reviews in largest probe by Singapore's competition watchdog

Forty-five companies must apologise publicly and remove fabricated reviews after admitting they bought bogus ratings from a single supplier to boost their online standing.

SINGAPORE: Ranging from restaurants to hair salons, 45 businesses will be required to apologise publicly after admitting to purchasing bogus reviews from a supplier to boost their online ratings, Singapore's competition watchdog said on Wednesday (Sep 30).

These companies have committed to stop using fake reviews, and will be required to remove existing ones and report their progress in doing so, along with other measures, the Competition and Consumer Commission of Singapore (CCS) said.

Calling the probe its largest concerning fake reviews to date, CCS said the businesses were found to have bought fabricated reviews from Mr Julian Tung Yan Kai, director of digital marketing firm Reputifly.

Companies that have purchased fake reviews

The first phase covered 47 companies — 45 of which admitted purchasing fake reviews. Among them are medical clinics, a funeral care firm, a law-linked consultancy, interior designers, restaurants and hair salons.

"They are not limited to any particular sector. They range from doctors, lawyers, tuition agencies, restaurants."

How the fake reviews were supplied

Reputifly operated two websites, BuyReviewSG and GetReviewSG, that posted fake reviews at periodic intervals. One package offered 35 five-star reviews over seven days for S$219.

The provider used generative AI to create reviews "designed to resemble genuine customer experiences", varying writing styles and including Singapore context and natural imperfections. It later built a dashboard to generate, manage and track fake reviews, drawing from existing four- and five-star Google reviews.

Telegram channels were used to recruit people to post the fabricated endorsements in exchange for payment.

Actions taken

The 45 firms must identify and remove fake reviews, report progress, strengthen compliance measures and post a prominent public apology for six months on their websites, social media accounts and premises.

Reputifly has admitted to aiding and abetting unfair trade practices and will donate proceeds from its fake-review service to a registered charity.
